Position-Based Hand Selection — Tighten Your RFI Ranges
preflop

RFI (Raise First In) is the most important decision you make in any hand. It's the only spot where you have 100% control — no one else has acted yet. Your RFI range determines everything that follows: your c-bet frequency, your defense range, and ultimately your profitability from each position.

The rule: your RFI range should tighten by roughly one hand class per seat moving from button to under the gun. On the button, you can open ~40% of hands (any pair, any suited ace, K9o+, Q9o+, J9o+, T9o+, suited connectors 65s+). From the cutoff, drop to ~30%. From hijack, ~22%. From under the gun, ~15% — only premium pairs (TT+), strong broadway (AJs+, KQs, AQo+), and good suited aces (ATs+).

Let's make this concrete with an example. You're in middle position with J9s (jack-nine suited). It looks playable — suited, semi-connected, feels like a leopard hand. But think about who's left to act: the cutoff, button, blinds. Any of them could wake up with AQ, KJs, or TT and dominate you. J9s from middle position has negative expected value long-term because you're out of position against too many stronger ranges. The correct play is fold. You're not being weak — you're respecting position.

This connects directly to your 57% JUNK%. Most of those junk hands enter the pot from early or middle position where even marginal hands are losing plays. The fix isn't complicated: print out an RFI chart by position and tape it to your monitor or chip tray. Before you open, check: "Am I in the right position for this hand?" If the answer is no, fold. The leopard strikes from the tall grass of late position — not from the open field of early position. Give yourself the positional advantage before you even look at your cards.
